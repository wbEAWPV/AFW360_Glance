The official national poverty and inequality statistics for Senegal are derived from the second round of the Enquête Harmonisée sur les Conditions de Vie des Ménages (EHCVM), a harmonized survey program across WAEMU countries, conducted by the Agence Nationale de la Statistique et de la Démographie (ANSD). The latest household survey was carried out between November 2021 and September 2022 (EHCVM 2021), and the data is comparable to the EHCVM 2018. The EHCVM is a nationally representative survey of 7,100 households, which also represents the geopolitical zones at both urban and rural levels. Survey microdata is publicly available through the World Bank Microdata Library. The next round of the EHCVM is scheduled for 2025.

The welfare metric used to estimate poverty is consumption per capita. The food poverty line reflects the cost of consuming 2,100 kilocalories per person per day, while the total poverty line is estimated to cover the expenditure necessary to meet non-food needs. The cost-of-the-basic-needs poverty line, estimated using the EHCVM 2018, was CFA 519.8 per capita per day in current prices. This resulted in a national headcount rate of 37.5 percent in 2021. International poverty rates use consumption per capita, adjusted for temporal and spatial cost-of-living differences across agroecological zones and areas of residence. Welfare projections beyond 2021 are based on microsimulations that rely on EHCVM 2021 microdata and macroeconomic data, including sectoral and GDP growth and inflation rates.
